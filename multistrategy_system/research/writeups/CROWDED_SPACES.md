# Crowded Spaces on S&P 500 anomaly legs (2026-09-16)

Research only: `crowded_spaces.py`, gate pre-registered in its docstring; `parse_13f.py` reduces
the SEC Form 13F structured datasets. Registry T-2026-09-16-35 (paper construction), -36
(unfiltered control), -37 (mirror). Data: 28 quarterly 13F archives 2019Q4–2026Q2
(`backtest_data/sec13f/`, 2.6 GB), CUSIP map from SEC fails-to-deliver files
(`backtest_data/cusip_map_sp500.csv`, 618 of 625 names), holdings per stock per quarter
(`results/inst_holdings_sp500.csv.gz`), the dated S&P 500 universe and 25-year IBKR prices.
Outputs `results/crowded_spaces_panel.csv`, `crowded_spaces_daily.csv`.

**The claim, from the abstract (Crossref, verbatim):** "anomaly risk-adjusted returns are
primarily generated by the most (least) crowded stocks for the long-leg (short-leg) portfolio",
significant after publication, 1980–2021. The 2026-09-02 scan had summarised this backwards
("long uncrowded / short crowded"); corrected before the gate was written.

**Answer: not on S&P 500 names since 2020.** The paper's construction loses 10% a year net and
is worse than the unfiltered anomaly legs it filters. Every gate criterion fails. The mirror
construction is the only positive line, which is informational and not adoptable from this run.

## Construction

Crowding: institutional days to liquidate, DTL = aggregate 13F share holdings (original 13F-HR
filings, share positions only, filed within 60 days of the quarter end) divided by FINRA average
daily volume, available 46 days after the quarter end. Median DTL 124 days; median 792 managers
per stock; institutional ownership median 84% of shares (informational measure IO).

Anomaly legs, quintiles within the dated universe at each month-end, each signed so the long leg
is the predicted-high side: 12-1 momentum, one-month reversal, low idiosyncratic volatility, low
net issuance, low short interest, 20-year same-month seasonality. Pooled long leg = in at least
one top quintile and no bottom quintile; short leg the mirror. CS = most-crowded tercile of the
long leg against least-crowded tercile of the short leg. ALL = the whole legs. REV_CS = the
opposite terciles. Equal weight, monthly, 10 bp per unit of one-way turnover.

## Reading

- **The crowded long-leg stocks were the ones that crashed.** The paper's own mechanism is
  that crowded anomaly positions carry crash risk that institutions are paid for. In 2020–2026
  the crash risk was realised: the most-crowded long-leg names in these anomalies (large,
  heavily held momentum and low-vol stocks) underperformed, and the least-crowded short-leg
  names (unloved stocks institutions had left) recovered. That is the risk showing up, not the
  premium.
- **The mirror at +0.44 is what "sell what institutions crowd into" looks like after 2020.**
  It was registered as an informational configuration, it is the opposite of the pre-stated
  claim, and its own last two years are −0.87. It is not a candidate; if a mechanism for it is
  proposed later it needs a blind test that is not 2020–2026 on this universe, which would mean
  the 2013–2019 13F archives (not downloaded).
- **The price-only anomalies on this universe, unfiltered, are dead or worse**: momentum flat,
  one-month reversal −0.59, low idiosyncratic vol −0.49. That closes the residual-momentum and
  reversal shortlist items for S&P 500 names without spending trials on them.
- **On the week's pattern**: this is the fifth cross-sectional large-cap test with a
  pre-registered gate, and the fifth to find the pre-2020 effect absent or reversed since.

## Data notes

- Institutional holdings are aggregated across all 13F filers, not the anomaly-following subset
  the paper may use; a manager-level version would need the paper's full text, which is behind
  Elsevier and SSRN.
- Filings made more than 60 days after the quarter end are dropped (1–2% of shares); without
  that rule, filings with period dates years in the past leaked in and the manager counts were
  wrong (first run: median 13 managers per stock; corrected: 792). Results barely moved.
- One archive nests its tables in a folder; the parser finds them by name.
- The 13F data go back to 2013 on the SEC site; only 2019Q4 onward was fetched.

## Consequence

Not a candidate. The 13F pipeline, the CUSIP map and the holdings file are reusable. Registry at
37 trials, two survivors.

> **Audit fix 2026-10-11 (finding 11).** Holdings now count only 13F filings published before the date each quarter is first used. 8,246 late manager filings (0.01% of shares) were removed. The rerun is identical to the decimal (CS −0.59, ALL −0.35, mirror +0.44), so the verdict stands on point-in-time data.
